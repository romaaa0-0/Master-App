# Lecture 1: Introduction to the Chinese Financial System

> **In one sentence:** finance is the movement of money from people who have more than they spend to people who need more than they have, and this lecture builds the map (assets, markets, institutions) that the rest of the course hangs on.

## 1. What finance is

Finance is the **flow of funds between sectors** of the economy.

- **Surplus sectors** earn more than they spend (usually **households**).
- **Shortage sectors** spend more than they earn (usually **firms and government**).
- Money moves between them in two ways:
  - **Fiscal transformation**: the government taxes the surplus and spends it.
  - **Financial transformation**: banks and other institutions link the two sides through deposits, loans and financial products.

### Direct vs. indirect finance

| | Direct finance | Indirect finance |
|---|---|---|
| How | Savers fund borrowers directly | Funds pass through an intermediary |
| Instruments | Stocks, bonds | Deposits, loans |
| Where | Stock and bond markets | Banks, insurers, funds, trusts |

**Key insight:** markets and intermediaries are **complements, not substitutes**. Markets *price and trade* claims; intermediaries *pool, transform and monitor* risk.

## 2. Financial assets, instruments and products

- **Asset**: anything valuable in exchange. **Tangible** (real, physical) or **intangible** (patents, goodwill, and **financial assets**).
- **Financial asset**: a claim that entitles you to cash (or another financial asset) from someone else. Its value depends on the issuer's **ability and willingness to pay**.
- **Financial instrument**: the **legal contract** that creates the claim. An asset for one side, a liability or equity claim for the other (cash, shares, bonds, derivatives).
- **Financial product**: a **standardized offering** built on instruments and packaged for investors (mutual funds, ETFs, insurance, structured deposits).

**One security, three identities:** an *instrument* to the issuer, an *asset* to the holder, a *product* in the market.

**Why standardization matters:** uniform contracts (e.g. a Treasury bond has the same maturity, coupon and par for everyone), transparent pricing, and easy trading in secondary markets.

### Classifying instruments

- **By duration:** *money market* (< 1 year: T-bills, commercial paper, CDs) vs. *capital market* (> 1 year: stocks, bonds, mortgages).
- **By nature:** *debt* (fixed claims: bonds, loans), *equity* (residual ownership: stocks), *derivatives* (payoff tied to an underlying: futures, options).

## 3. Financial markets

A financial market is a platform where buyers and sellers trade financial assets.

**Five functions:** (1) turn savings into investment, (2) provide **liquidity**, (3) **price discovery**, (4) cut transaction costs, (5) spread and transfer **risk**.

**Three components:** *subjects* (participants), *objects* (instruments traded), *mechanisms* (rules, platforms, price formation).

### Six ways to classify markets

| By | Categories |
|---|---|
| Object traded | Money market · Capital market · FX market · Gold market |
| Financing method | Direct (stocks, bonds) · Indirect (bank loans) |
| Transaction level | **Primary** (new issues, capital formation) · **Secondary** (resale, liquidity) |
| Pricing | Open (bidding, exchanges) · Negotiated (OTC) |
| Organization | Visible (centralized exchanges) · Invisible (dealer networks, FX, OTC) |
| Delivery timing | Spot (now) · Derivative (future delivery) |

### China's market landscape

Money market, bond market, stock market, FX, commodity futures, financial derivatives, VC/PE, insurance and funds, gold.

- **Money market:** short-term, low risk, low return, very liquid, huge volume. Players: banks, the central bank, government, corporates. Instruments: T-bills, commercial paper, bank acceptance drafts, negotiable CDs, **repos**.
- **Capital market:** long-term funding. In China its two pillars are **equities** and **long-term bonds**.
- **FX market:** global, decentralized, 24 hours, the largest and most liquid market.
- **Derivatives:** forwards, futures, options, swaps. **OTC** is bigger but less regulated; **exchange-traded** is more transparent. Functions: **hedging, speculation, price discovery, arbitrage**.

## 4. Financial institutions

Two broad classes: **regulators** and **intermediaries**.

### Regulators in China

- **PBOC** (People's Bank of China): central bank, monetary policy.
- **CSRC**: securities and futures.
- **NFRA** (National Financial Regulatory Administration): banking and insurance.
- **SAFE**: FX, reserves, capital-account rules (administered by the PBOC).
- Since **2023**, the **Central Financial Commission** (Party level) coordinates above them. The **Ministry of Finance** owns the state's bank capital via **Central Huijin**.

### Intermediaries in China

China's system is built around **large state-owned commercial banks**, with many other institutions alongside.

- **PBOC**: the monetary authority, *not* a bank for the public (no public deposits, no commercial loans). It issues currency, manages money supply, is the bank of the government and of banks, and guards stability. *Its liability is the currency everyone holds, which is why it can set the price of money.*
- **Policy banks**: serve national goals, not profit; funded by the state and bonds, not deposits.
- **Commercial banks**: take deposits, make loans, and **create money** (loans create new deposits).
- **Non-bank institutions:**
  1. **Securities companies** (= investment banks in the US): brokerage, dealing, underwriting.
  2. **Fund management companies**: pool money, professionally managed, diversified.
  3. **Stock exchanges**: not-for-profit, self-regulating venues. **Shanghai (1990)**, **Shenzhen (1990, tech/growth)**, **Beijing (2021, innovative SMEs)**.
  4. **Asset management companies (AMCs)**: the four big ones were created in the late 1990s to absorb **bad loans (NPLs)** from state banks.
  5. **Insurance companies**: pool risk and invest premiums as big institutional investors.
  6. **Trust companies**: manage assets under trust agreements. Officially "no deposits", but in practice trust plans acted like **deposits with implicit guarantees**.
  7. **Financial leasing companies**: buy assets and lease them to firms.
  8. **Finance companies**: "internal banks" for big enterprise groups, no public deposits.

### Seven functions of intermediaries

Channel funds · cut transaction costs · provide liquidity · produce information (screen and monitor borrowers) · transform risk · **transform maturity** (borrow short, lend long) · run payments.

## 5. The map for the whole course

**Authorities on top** (PBOC, SAFE, NFRA, CSRC, coordinated by the Central Financial Commission), **markets** (money, bond, stock, VC/PE, derivatives, FX) and **institutions** (banking, securities, insurance, trust, others) underneath.

## Key terms to know

Surplus/shortage sector · direct vs. indirect finance · instrument / asset / product · money vs. capital market · primary vs. secondary · OTC · spot vs. derivative · PBOC, CSRC, NFRA, SAFE · policy bank · AMC · NPL · maturity transformation
