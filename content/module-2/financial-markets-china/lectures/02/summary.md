# Lecture 2: Chinese Economy and Financial Sectors

> **In one sentence:** China's financial system is not a delayed copy of the West; it is the institutional answer to a specific growth model, and it is being rebuilt as that model changes.

## 1. Where China stands today (2024)

| Indicator | Value | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| GDP | **$18.8 trillion** | #2 |
| Trade | $6.1 trillion | #1 |
| FX reserves | $3.2 trillion | #1 |
| Population | 1.41 billion (~20% of world) | |
| Share of global manufacturing | ~30% | |
| Labor force | 780 million | #1 |

**But** GDP per person is only about **$13,400** (around 70th in the world).

### Structural shift (share of GDP)

| | 1952 | 2000 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agriculture | 51% | 15% | 7% |
| Industry | 21% | 46% | 38% |
| Services | 28% | 39% | 55% |

Agrarian society → world factory → service economy. Moving workers from low- to high-productivity sectors was *itself* a source of growth.

### How unusual the growth was

- GDP went from **$59.7B (1960)** to **$18.8T (2024)**: about **315x** in nominal dollars (roughly **100 to 120x** in real terms).
- Around **10% a year** from 1970 to 2010; around **5%** now.
- **800M+** people lifted out of extreme poverty. Overtook Japan as #2 in **2010**.

## 2. Before 1978: the planned economy

**In 1949** China was one of the poorest countries on earth: GDP per person ~$50–60, 85–90% rural, life expectancy 35–40, literacy 20–30%, steel output just 158,000 tons (vs. 1 billion tons in 2024).

**Why planning?** A war-torn, farming nation with almost no industry chose a planned economy built on: state resource allocation, **collectivized agriculture**, and a **heavy-industry-first** strategy. Results: rationing and a **shortage economy**.

**Three phases:** reconstruction and the First Five-Year Plan → Great Leap Forward and the Great Famine (misallocation) → Cultural Revolution (stagnation).

### Why reform became unavoidable in 1978

1. **Very low growth:** GDP per person **$156**, just 1.8% of world output (vs. US $10,500, Japan $8,800, South Korea $1,400, India $206).
2. **Agriculture could not feed 790 million people** (~0.1 hectare of land each, rationing).
3. **Inefficient industry:** SOEs made 77.6% of output, with **soft budget constraints** and no incentives.
4. **Big technology gap:** R&D under 1% of GDP, modern sectors almost absent.

## 3. What 1978 changed

**Method:** "It doesn't matter if the cat is black or white", "crossing the river by feeling for the stones". Incremental, experimental, pilot-based.

### The four pillars of reform

| Pillar | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| ① **Rural reform** | **Household responsibility system**, started secretly by 18 families in **Xiaogang village** (1978). Grain 300M → 400M tons by 1984; farm incomes doubled in 6 years. | Broke through first because the crisis was deepest. Freed labor for township enterprises. |
| ② **SOE reform** | Decentralization (78–84) → tax-for-profit and contracting (84–92) → modern enterprise system, "**grasp the big, release the small**", Zhu Rongji layoffs (93–03) → **SASAC** and listings (03–13) → mixed ownership (2013–). | Took 30 years; each step fixed the last one's soft budget problem and created new ones. |
| ③ **Price reform** | **Dual-track pricing**: plan prices for quota output, market prices for extra output. The **1988 "price breakthrough"** failed and caused panic buying. Mostly unified after 1992. | Let prices be discovered without a shock, but the gap between tracks created **rent-seeking**. |
| ④ **Opening up** | **SEZs** in 1980 (Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Shantou, Xiamen) → 14 coastal cities (1984) → Pudong (1990) → **WTO (2001)** → Shanghai FTZ (2013), RCEP, Belt and Road. | "**Reform through opening**": outside rules forced domestic reform. |

## 4. The other growth engines

- **Demographic dividend:** working-age population 400M (1980) → 900M (2010); the dividend faded after 2010.
- **High savings:** national saving above **40%** of GDP (precautionary saving because of weak social security; high corporate retained earnings). **Savings → bank loans → investment** is the cash loop of a bank-led system.
- **Exports:** ~20% annual growth after WTO; 30% of world manufacturing; built $3.2T of reserves. Slowed after 2008.
- **Urbanization:** 18% (1978) → **67%** (2024); urban population 170M → 930M. Driven by housing reform and **land finance**.
- **Infrastructure:** 45,000 km of high-speed rail, 180,000 km of expressways. Lowered costs but built up **local government debt**.

### "56789": who really produces the output

Private firms provide **50%+ of tax, 60%+ of GDP, 70%+ of innovation, 80%+ of urban jobs, 90%+ of firms**. Yet credit is allocated mostly through a **state-owned** banking system.

> **Hold this for the whole course:** output is produced privately; credit is allocated by the state. Banks, bonds, shadow banking and P2P are all attempts to bridge that gap, and every failure in the course is a failure to bridge it.

## 5. How the financial system evolved

**Before 1978, there was no real financial system.** The PBOC was central bank, commercial bank and cashier at once. Investment came from **fiscal grants** that never had to be repaid. No bond, stock or insurance markets. Money was a unit of account, not an allocator.

### Six stages (each one triggered by a crisis)

| Period | Stage | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1949–78 | Monolithic banking | Fiscal cashier, no markets |
| 1979–84 | Restoration | Four specialized state banks restored; **grants became loans** |
| 1985–93 | Probing marketization | Central bank separated; stock exchanges born; two inflations |
| 1994–2001 | Legal reform | PBOC Law and Commercial Bank Law; **bad loans stripped out** to AMCs |
| 2002–15 | Post-WTO expansion | Banks listed; **4-trillion-yuan stimulus**; shadow banking rises |
| 2017–now | Risk control | Asset-management rules, deleveraging, fintech oversight |

**Thread:** financial reform is **problem-driven, not design-driven**.

### Today's architecture (three layers)

1. **Direction:** Central Financial Commission (Party level).
2. **Regulation:** PBOC (monetary and macro-prudential, with SAFE), NFRA (banks, insurance), CSRC (securities, futures, funds).
3. **Operations:** institutions (the **big six state banks**: ICBC, ABC, BOC, CCB, BoCom, PSBC; 12 joint-stock banks; city and rural banks; brokers, insurers, trusts, AMCs, fintech) and markets (interbank money market, bond market where the **interbank market handles 90%+**, stock exchanges SSE/SZSE/BSE, FX, gold, derivatives).

**Bank-led** (indirect finance is about **two thirds**), state-controlled, increasingly coordinated.

### Six key features

① High saving rate · ② bank dominance · ③ state control of credit · ④ capital controls (managed float, QFII/QDII quotas) · ⑤ segmented regulation, now converging · ⑥ fintech leadership (mobile payment, digital yuan).

These are **rational choices for a stage of development**, not flaws. The question is when they work and when they turn into risk.

## 6. The costs of high growth

1. **Inequality:** Gini 0.31 (1980) → peak 0.49 → ~0.47.
2. **Pollution:** heavy emissions before the 2010s; "dual-carbon" goals since 2020.
3. **Debt (key):** non-financial corporate leverage ~**160% of GDP**; hidden local government debt estimated at **60 to 70 trillion yuan**; property adjustment risk.
4. **Financial fragility (key):** shadow banking, channel business, concentration in property loans, weak small and mid-size banks.

## 7. The logic behind Chinese policy decisions

| Component | How it works | Theory |
|---|---|---|
| ① **Pilot first** | SEZs, FTZs, pilot cities generate information; scale up what works | Answers **Hayek's knowledge problem** |
| ② **Fiscal decentralization** | Local governments compete for investment; GDP-based promotion "tournament" | **Blanchard & Shleifer (2001)**; but causes duplication and local debt |
| ③ **Dual-track transition** | Protect the existing stock, liberalize the increment | Reduces resistance, Pareto improvements; but creates rents |

Three questions behind every reform: **information** (who knows?), **political feasibility** (will it survive existing coalitions?), **cost allocation** (who pays decides the next reform).
