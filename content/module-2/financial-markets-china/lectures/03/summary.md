# Lecture 3: Central Bank and Monetary Policy in China

> **In one sentence:** the PBOC is a central bank that does more jobs than the Fed or ECB, with more tools, because for most of its history the "price channel" (interest rates) didn't work well, so quantity tools had to do the work.

## 1. What a central bank does

**Standard functions:** issue the only legal currency · be the **bankers' bank** (lender of last resort) · be the government's bank and run payments · conduct monetary policy · supervise.

**Characteristics:** not for profit, limited direct business, government privileges.

**What makes the PBOC different:**
- **Multiple objectives**, not just price stability.
- Sits **under the State Council**, not independent.
- **Bank lending** is almost the whole transmission channel.
- Kept **quantity tools** long after rich countries moved to price tools.
- **Two extra jobs:** manages the **exchange rate** and the **capital account**. That is why its toolbox is so long.

### The PBOC today

| | |
|---|---|
| Mandate | "Maintain the stability of the value of the currency and thereby promote economic growth" (PBOC Law 1995, amended 2003) |
| Governance | Ministry-level body under the State Council; the Central Financial Commission sets direction |
| Branches | 31 provincial + 5 city branches + ~300 city level (the 9 regional branches of 1998 were abolished in **2023**) |
| Inside it | SAFE, the Digital Currency Institute (**e-CNY**), **CIPS** cross-border payments, credit registry, bond settlement |
| Balance sheet | ~**RMB 42 trillion** (end 2024) |

## 2. How the PBOC's role evolved (7 stages)

| Stage | Years | What happened | Key line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1948–78 | **Monobank.** Founded 1 Dec 1948 in Shijiazhuang (merger of 3 banks). One bank matching credit to the output plan. | A coherent framework for planning, not a crude precursor. |
| 2 | 1979–84 | **Four state banks split off.** "Loan-for-grant" reform. **ICBC** carved out in 1984 to take over PBOC's commercial lending. | The split created banks; it did not yet create banking. |
| 3 | 1985–94 | **Two inflations.** 1988 (~18%) and the 1992–94 credit surge (CPI peaked ~**24%** in 1994). Local branches lent to local projects. **1993 Decision** on financial reform. | The second inflation, not the first, produced the legal framework. |
| 4 | 1995 | **PBOC Law.** Price stability first; **Article 29**: no overdraft to the government, no direct purchase of Treasury debt. | Real independence = the ban on financing the deficit directly. After 2008 the workaround was **LGFVs** borrowing from banks. |
| 5 | 1998 | **Nine cross-regional branches** replaced provincial ones to stop local capture; **credit quotas abolished**. | Lasted 25 years; reversed in 2023. |
| 6 | 2003–17 | **Bank supervision spun off** to the CBRC. PBOC focuses on monetary policy and stability. | Created gaps between macro and micro supervision, which helped shadow banking grow. |
| 7 | 2017– | **Coordination era.** FSDC (2017), CBRC + CIRC → CBIRC (2018), CBIRC → **NFRA** (2023); PBOC gains macro-prudential powers. | Add coordination rather than undo separation. |

**Logic:** separate functions when role conflicts become costly; coordinate again when separation costs become visible. **Reform is crisis-driven, not scheduled** (1983 fiscal pressure, 1993 inflation, 1997–98 Asian crisis).

## 3. China's monetary system

- **RMB** is the only legal currency, issued only by the PBOC; foreign currency can't circulate domestically.

### Money aggregates (China publishes M0, M1, M2)

| | Definition | ~Size end 2024 | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M0** | Cash outside banks | ~12 tn | Only aggregate the PBOC fully controls |
| **M1** | M0 + corporate demand deposits (+ since **Dec 2024**: household demand deposits and payment-institution reserves) | | Transaction money, most cyclical |
| **M2** | M1 + time and savings deposits + most other deposits | ~**313 tn** | Broad money; no longer an official target |

- **M1–M2 "scissors gap":** M1 growing faster = money being *activated*; M2 growing faster = money being *hoarded* in savings.
- **Why M1 was redefined:** mobile payments moved spending money out of the old definition; old M1 fell for 18 months while retail sales grew. Redefinition changes measurement, not behaviour.
- **M2/GDP ≈ 232%** (vs. US ~72%): bank-dominated system, ~45% saving rate, few alternative assets, repressed deposit rates. Not automatically inflationary, but means more capital per unit of GDP (rising ICOR). Stopped rising after 2017.

### The PBOC balance sheet: three eras

| Era | What drove money creation |
|---|---|
| **2003–2014: FX accumulation** | Foreign assets up to ~27 tn. PBOC bought dollars from exporters and **sterilized** with central-bank bills and **RRR hikes**. |
| **2015–2019: lending to banks** | FX plateaued; **MLF, PSL, relending** became the injection channel (claims on banks ~2.5 → ~18 tn). |
| **2020s: structural tools** | Targeted relending (green, tech, small firms, rental housing) + a price corridor. |

**MLF** = Medium-term Lending Facility (for commercial banks). **PSL** = Pledged Supplementary Lending (for policy banks).

## 4. Monetary policy framework

**Three elements:** objectives · instruments · transmission.

- **Objectives:** price stability, growth, employment, balance of payments, financial stability. China pursues **several at once** with no published ranking.
- **Instruments:** *quantity* (RRR, credit quotas, relending) · *price* (policy rates, OMO rates, LPR) · *administrative* (**window guidance**, selective credit controls).
- **Transmission:** policy rate → interbank rate → deposit and lending rates → activity. Each link can leak.

| Model | Objectives | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Single mandate | Inflation | ECB, many inflation targeters |
| Dual mandate | Inflation + employment | Fed |
| Multiple objectives | Prices, growth, jobs, external balance, stability | PBOC |

### Five phases of monetary policy

| Phase | Period | Signature |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1949–78 | Extension of the fiscal plan: credit quotas, mandatory lending |
| 2 | 1979–92 | Quotas kept; pilot RRR and relending |
| 3 | 1993–2002 | **Market-based turn.** 1998: **credit quotas abolished** (birth of modern Chinese monetary policy); M2 target; OMO and RRR |
| 4 | 2003–15 | **Sterilization era.** RRR raised from ~6% (2003) to ~**21%** (2011): "quantity absorbing quantity" |
| 5 | 2016– | **Dual pillar:** monetary policy + **macro-prudential** (MPA) |

### 2015: the turning point

- **August 2015 "811" FX reform:** fixing more market-based, one-off ~3% devaluation, capital outflows followed.
- **2015–16 stock crash:** Shanghai index −45%; financial stability becomes an active mandate.
- **Interest-rate corridor** formalized.

### The interest-rate corridor

- **Ceiling:** SLF (emergency lending rate).
- **Policy rate:** 7-day reverse repo (July 2024 – Oct 2026) → **overnight reverse repo at 1.25% since 2 Nov 2026**.
- **Market rate:** **DR001**, the overnight interbank repo rate, moves inside the corridor.
- **Floor:** **IOER** (interest on excess reserves), 0.35%.

### 2019 LPR reform: the "last mile"

Problem: PBOC cut rates but corporate borrowing costs barely moved (banks priced off the old benchmark). Fix: **LPR** (quoted monthly by 20 banks) linked to the policy rate; new loans must price off LPR. Result: **policy rate → LPR → lending rate**. Still unfinished: **deposit rates** remain coordinated, not competed.

### Structural tools (2020–)

Targeted, collateralized, cheap relending to green, SMEs, tech, agriculture, rental housing. Essentially **fiscal policy run through the central bank's balance sheet**: they can steer credit to a sector with demand, but can't create demand where balance sheets are damaged.

### Instruments at a glance

| Instrument | Type |
|---|---|
| Reserve requirement (RRR) | Quantity |
| Policy rates (overnight / 7-day reverse repo) | Price |
| MLF | Price + quantity |
| Open market operations | Quantity |
| Window guidance | Quantity (administrative) |
| Prudential leverage rules | Quantity |

The list is long **because the price channel was weak**.

### Why the price channel is weak

Policy rate → deposit rate (administered, sticky) → lending rate (negotiated with state-linked borrowers) → activity (**guaranteed borrowers ignore price**). **Soft budget constraints** are an institutional problem, not a technical one.

## 5. Open economy: the impossible trinity

You can have only two of: **independent monetary policy**, **fixed/managed exchange rate**, **free capital flows**.

- **China 1994–2015:** kept monetary autonomy and a managed exchange rate; gave up free capital flows (**capital controls**).
- **After 2015:** more FX flexibility; controls remain but more targeted.
- Reserve accumulation and RRR hikes were the **domestic cost of buying exchange-rate stability**.
- **Internal vs. external stability:** cutting rates to fight deflation pressures the currency. China resolves this with the **capital account**, not the interest rate.

## 6. PBOC vs. Fed / ECB

| | Fed / ECB | PBOC |
|---|---|---|
| Independence | Operationally independent | Within the State Council |
| Objectives | Mostly price stability (Fed: dual) | Growth, jobs, prices, external balance, stability |
| Toolkit | Policy rate + QE/QT | Price, quantity, administrative, structural |

## 7. Five takeaways

1. **Multiple objectives** are rational for a developing economy, but create conflicts and opacity.
2. **Old and new tools coexist** because of real transmission gaps, not inertia.
3. The framework must **match market conditions**, not a textbook ideal.
4. **Gradualism is a feature**, not a bug.
5. China challenges the idea that a single-objective, price-only framework is always best during development.
