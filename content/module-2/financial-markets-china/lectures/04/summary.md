# Lecture 4: Financial Regulation System in China

> **In one sentence:** China's regulatory map was not designed in advance; it was compiled after the fact, with each new regulator created to fix a failure the previous structure couldn't see. **Regulation follows accidents.**

## 1. Today's regulators

A financial regulator supervises the system according to law. Three jobs: **supervise markets**, **inspect institutions**, **issue rules**.

**Current structure: "one bank, one administration, one commission"** (一行一局一会), with the **Central Financial Commission** (Party level, 2023) on top.

| Regulator | Since | Covers | Key roles |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PBOC** | 1948 (pure central bank after 1983) | Monetary policy, currency, financial stability | Bank of issue · bank of the government · bank of banks |
| **NFRA** | May 2023 (replaced CBIRC) | Banks, insurers, all non-securities finance | **Licensing**, **prudential rules** (capital, risk, governance), **consumer protection** (can fine mis-selling) |
| **CSRC** | Oct 1992 | Securities and futures | IPO vetting (now **registration-based**), continuous disclosure, **delisting** for fraud or losses |
| **SAFE** | 1979 (administered by PBOC) | FX | Balance-of-payments statistics, FX market, foreign debt, **manages FX reserves** |
| **Ministry of Finance** | (quasi-regulator) | | Accounting standards, **capital injections** into state banks, owns state shares |

### SAFE and the capital account

- **Current account:** RMB **freely convertible** for trade.
- **Capital account:** still **closed** to prevent capital flight.
- Openings without full convertibility: **Stock Connect, Bond Connect**, cross-border interbank pools.

### Why NFRA was needed: the "deposit-to-policy" scandal

Insurance agents posed as bank staff and sold **universal life insurance** as if it were a high-yield **deposit**, especially to the elderly. Surrender penalties and cooling-off periods were hidden. Separate bank and insurance regulators couldn't police a product sold across both.

### Two tiers

- **National:** PBOC, NFRA, CSRC, SAFE license and supervise banks, insurers, brokers and systemically important institutions.
- **Local** (provincial and municipal financial bureaus): supervise the **fringe**: microcredit, financing guarantees, pawnshops, leasing, factoring, local AMCs, regional trading venues. Weakness: uneven capacity and partly answerable to **local government**.

## 2. Evolution of regulation

### 1949–1977: centralized control

The PBOC did everything (central and commercial bank, state finances) under the State Council, alongside the Ministry of Finance. No markets, no independent oversight. **Rational at the time:** no private property, massive industrial projects to finance, no tax or savings base. **Policy loans replaced profit-driven lending.**

### 1978–1992: reform begins

- Specialized state banks restored: **ICBC, ABC, BOC, CCB**.
- **1986** Interim Regulations formalize the PBOC as central bank.
- **1990–91:** Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges open, mainly to raise private capital for **SOE recapitalization**. Experimental, hybrid model.
- **1992: CSRC.** In August 1992, **millions queued in Shenzhen** for IPO lottery tickets; unfair allocation triggered riots and lost confidence. Manipulation, insider trading and forged IPO papers were common. October 1992: State Council Securities Commission + CSRC created.
  > *The CSRC was not the market's designer; it was the ticket-taker after the market fell into disorder.*

### 1993–2000: building the legal framework

- **1995:** **PBOC Law** (no government overdrafts, price stability first) and **Commercial Bank Law**.
- **1998:** Securities Commission merged into the **CSRC** (unified securities and futures supervision).
- **1998: CIRC** created (18 Nov). Insurance had grown fast with mis-selling, claims problems and **interest-rate spread losses** (high guaranteed rates met rate cuts). Insurance risk (mortality, long-term promises, surrender) is different from bank risk.
  > *Insurance risk could no longer be managed with banking tools.*

### 2001–2017: specialized supervision

- **2001 WTO entry** forced: lifting foreign-ownership caps, ending geographic limits, **Basel capital standards**, faster **NPL write-offs**, recapitalizing the big four, independent directors. Done within the **5-year transition**.
- **2003: CBRC** created, ending the conflict between the PBOC as operator and supervisor. → **"One bank, three commissions"** (PBOC + CBRC + CIRC + CSRC).
- **2008 crisis → credit boom → shadow banking** (wealth-management products, trust loans, entrusted loans). The fragmented structure couldn't see cross-sector products → **regulatory arbitrage**.
- **2015** stock crash.
- **2017: FSDC** (Financial Stability and Development Committee) under the State Council to coordinate.

### 2018–present: integrated supervision

- **2018: CBRC + CIRC → CBIRC** → "**one bank, two commissions**". Ended duplicate rules on bancassurance, unified capital rules for financial holding companies. A shift from **institutional** to **functional** regulation.
- **2018 New Asset Management Rules** (27 Apr, PBOC + CBRC + CSRC + SAFE): the legal backbone of the **shadow-banking crackdown**.
  - **Break implicit guarantees** ("rigid repayment"); products must use **net value, mark-to-market**.
  - Cut **channel business** and **nested products**; same rules for similar products across banks, insurers, brokers, funds.
  - Effect: bank WMPs and trust channels shrank; risk moved back **onto balance sheets**.
- **MPA (Macro-Prudential Assessment):** dampens credit booms (procyclicality) and limits arbitrage through shadow channels.
- **2023: NFRA** absorbs CBIRC (and other functions): one mega-regulator for all non-securities finance.
- **2023: Central Financial Commission** (Party) replaces the FSDC; plus a **Central Financial Work Commission** for Party building and personnel. **Coordination moved from negotiation to command.**

### Timeline at a glance

| Year | Change | Triggered by |
|---|---|---|
| 1992 | CSRC | Market chaos, forged IPO documents |
| 1998 | CIRC | Insurance mis-selling and solvency problems |
| 2003 | CBRC | Separate bank supervision from monetary policy |
| 2017 | FSDC | Shadow banking, 2015 crash |
| 2018 | CBIRC | Bancassurance mis-selling, cross-sector products |
| 2023 | NFRA + CFC | Risks in local debt, property, small banks |

## 3. Challenges today

1. **Fragmented coordination:** gaps remain between PBOC, CSRC and local authorities; limited data sharing and joint enforcement.
2. **Support vs. prevention:** regulators must promote growth *and* contain leverage, property and local debt → policy swings.
3. **Shadow banking and new gaps:** off-balance-sheet financing, trusts, **LGFVs**; new gaps in private funds, supply-chain finance, informal lending.
4. **Fintech outpaced regulation:** data governance, cybersecurity, algorithm transparency.
5. **Weak local supervision:** uneven, short of talent.
6. **Reactive, not preventive:** little use of big data, AI, early warning.

## 4. Trends

1. **Fragmented → unified** supervision (NFRA covers almost everything except securities).
2. **Macro-prudential** focus (MPA; shadow banking, property, local debt).
3. **Consumer protection** (dedicated bureaus).
4. **Rule-based enforcement** (revised PBOC Law, upcoming **Financial Stability Law**).
5. **RegTech** (real-time data; regulating fintech, e-CNY, big platforms like **Ant Group**).
6. **Real economy and green finance** (SMEs, tech, ESG, green bonds).

## 5. The big lesson: regulation follows accidents

Why the pattern repeats (and why it's rational):

1. **Information constraint:** regulators can't see the whole balance sheet until a product spans two licenses. The 2018 merger was about **information**, not tidiness.
2. **Institutional interest:** each regulator served a constituency (local branches answered to local governments).
3. **Coordination cost:** committees share information; **command** coordinates action. Hence the 2023 CFC.

> *Fragmentation is not a design failure; it is the price of information and institutional interests.*
